# Bangladesh-first card refinement

## Scope and evidence

Reviewed on 2026-11-11. The user chose to preserve the shelf/lines structure and Bookplate, Poster, and Ticket, while refining boxes, spacing, overlaps, and the artwork. This is desk research and browser observation, not interviews, conversion evidence, or proof of what all Bangladeshi readers prefer.

## Channel context

[DataReportal, Digital 2026: Bangladesh](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-bangladesh) was opened successfully with gstack. The report explicitly says its 2026 edition uses data available in October 2025 and was published at the end of 2025.

- Internet users: 82.8 million; penetration 47.0%.
- Facebook potential advertising audience: 64.0 million in late 2025.
- Messenger advertising reach: 35.0 million in late 2025.
- Instagram potential advertising audience: 9.15 million in late 2025.
- X potential advertising audience: 1.17 million in late 2025.

Advertising reach is not monthly active users, unique people, book readers, or willingness to use Carried. Mobile connection totals are not smartphone-owner counts. These figures support investigating Facebook/messaging as acquisition contexts; they do not prove a design style or a specific age segment.

## Observed local interfaces

- [Rokomari](https://www.rokomari.com): inspected rendered navigation. Bengali labels appeared at 16px/24px, with some utility text at 13px/19.5px. Its declared font stack contains Latin and Bengali families. It is commerce-heavy, so its navigation density and promotional boxes are not a model for a personal card tool.
- [Boitoi](https://boitoi.com.bd): inspected English product headings with Bengali ebook/category content. The observed typography varied from 60px display to small book captions. This is evidence that bilingual presentation exists in local reading products, not proof that this hierarchy or caption size suits Carried.
- Earlier local font observations remain in [modern-bangla-typography.md](modern-bangla-typography.md). Keep the existing licensed Manrope/Anek Bangla pair rather than introduce another font based on superficial national associations.

## Design response

Working audience hypothesis: mobile readers sharing a personal Bengali or English reflection after reading. Validate this with reader sessions before making market claims.

1. The saved card, not a promotional panel, is the product. Refine quote/book/name hierarchy inside the existing editions.
2. Make Bengali conjuncts, vowel signs, mixed-script names, and full-content exports a visual acceptance requirement. Font loading alone is not shaping verification.
3. Offer direct writing access from existing mobile jump links. Keep language preferences device-local and voluntary.
4. Prioritize Save, copy, and native sharing. External X/WhatsApp links remain available but do not compete visually with the artwork action. No fake direct Messenger or Instagram integration.
5. Keep local drafts and recovery dependable. Do not add tracking, accounts, public test submissions, or fabricated community activity.
6. No flags, national-color decoration, nostalgia textures, or assumed cultural motif is added. Bangladesh-first means readable language and usable delivery, not a stereotype.

## Research gaps

No reader interviews, actual platform referral data, real-device native-share evaluation, or commercial audience validation were performed. W3C external fetches timed out during planning; do not cite them as inspected evidence. The implementation preserves the current language-detection behavior rather than claim a better default from country-level numbers.
